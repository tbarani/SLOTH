# test4 — 3D fission gas bubbles on the grain faces and grain edges of an extruded hexagonal polycrystal

3D version of the model of L.K. Aagesen, D. Schwen, M.R. Tonks, Y. Zhang, *Phase-field modeling
of fission gas bubble growth on grain boundaries and triple junctions in UO2 nuclear fuel*,
Comput. Mater. Sci. **161** (2019) 35-45 (the ScienceDirect link S0927025619300229), on the exact
microstructure of their 3D simulations (their Fig. 3): **four uniform hexagonal grains in the x-y
plane, extruded in z, fully periodic**. In 3D the grain boundaries are the grain *faces*
(vertical surfaces) and the triple junctions are the grain *edges* (the 8 vertical triple lines
of the 4-hexagon cell).

Same three-problem staggered model and same non-dimensionalised parameters as `test1`/`test2`
(Table 1 of Aagesen et al. 2019, with the single soft curvature k = 2.57e9 J/m3 = 0.04015625 of
D.-U. Kim et al., *Mater. Theory* 6 (2022) 7): lengths in nm, times in 0.1 s, energy densities in
64e9 J/m3. `coefficients.json` is generated by
`../test1/generate_coefficients.py -ng 4 --kvm 0.04015625 --kgm 0.04015625 --kvb 0.04015625 --kgb 0.04015625`.

## Initial conditions (Section 3.1 and Fig. 3 of the paper)

- Hexagons with edge-to-edge distance d = 0.6 um, i.e. circumradius a = d/sqrt(3) = 346.41 nm;
  periodic cell 1.039 x 1.2 x Lz um (our x is the paper's y). Reference case of Fig. 4/6c:
  Lz = 0.28 um, which gives NTJ = 1/lTJ = 7.14/um with **two bubbles on each of the eight
  triple lines** (lTJ = Lz/2 = 0.14 um, atoms at z = Lz/4 and 3Lz/4).
- **23 grain-boundary bubbles placed uniformly at random on the grain faces** with density
  NGB = 20/um2 (AGB = 12 * a * Lz = 2 sqrt(3) * 1.2 * 0.28 = 1.164 um2, cf. Table 2). The
  placement uses the analytic honeycomb (the 24 hexagon edge segments of the periodic cell,
  i.e. the 12 grain faces counted twice), not the mesh, so the centres lie exactly on the grain
  faces; it rejects centres closer than 2r (periodic images included) and uses a fixed seed so
  it is identical on every MPI rank and between runs.
- **All initial bubbles are spheres of radius 44 nm** (`-r`), as in the paper; they rapidly
  equilibrate to lenticular shapes on the grain faces and elongated triangular shapes on the
  triple lines.
- Options: `-ngb 23` (grain-face bubbles), `-ntj 2` (bubbles per triple line), `-r 44`.

## Mesh: from the hexagonal cell to `hex.msh`

`generate_mesh.sh` runs the three steps (all deterministic, the committed `hex.msh` is their
output):

1. `make_hex_geo.py` writes `hex3d.geo`. The geometry is built in an exact integer lattice
   (x = m*a/2, y = n*sqrt(3)*a/2, cell [0,6]x[0,4] i.e. [0,3a]x[0,2*sqrt(3)*a]): each hexagon of
   a patch is clipped to the cell (Sutherland-Hodgman, exact rational arithmetic), so the pieces
   tile the box exactly; opposite box edges carry identical line segments paired by
   `Periodic Line`, and the cell is extruded in z with `Extrude {0,0,Lz} { Surface{:};
   Layers{nz}; }`. Committed cell: a = 346.41 nm (d = 0.6 um), Lz = 280 nm, nz = 7 layers,
   lc = 40 nm base mesh (~41.5k tets, 6923 nodes after periodic merging). Production runs use
   `-amr -elm_amr 2` to reach the paper's ~10 nm element size at the interfaces.
2. `gmsh hex3d.geo -3 -format msh2 -setnumber Mesh.SaveAll 1 -o hex_raw.msh` meshes the extruded
   cell (conformal tets; gmsh 2.2 format, readable by MFEM 4.9 — MFEM's own `periodic-cube.msh`
   is 2.2 as well, so no need for gmsh format 4 / MFEM 4.10).
3. `finalize_mesh.py` rewrites `hex_raw.msh` into `hex.msh`: tetrahedra are tagged by centroid
   with the physical volume `grain_1..4` (dim-3 `$PhysicalNames`), boundary triangles on the box
   faces get the physical surface `box`, and a `$Periodic` section is appended **after
   `$Elements`** (three dim-2 blocks pairing the opposite box faces in x, y and z by
   nearest-neighbour node coordinates). The section order matters: MFEM 4.9's v2 gmsh reader
   applies the slave→master mapping only to the elements read *so far* and sets `curved=1`,
   which stores the slave vertex coordinates in the `Nodes` field (keeps wrapping elements
   geometrically correct). `gmsh -3` itself cannot produce the z-periodic extrusion here:
   `Periodic Surface` on the extruded top/bottom fails with "Cannot find periodic counterpart"
   (gmsh 4.12 without ANN support), so the xy periodicity comes from gmsh's `Periodic Line`
   propagation and the z pairs are computed from the node coordinates.

   The script also checks the mesh conformity (every tet face shared by at most 2 tets,
   single-owned faces = boundary triangles) and the grain assignment. The loaded mesh is
   checked to be exact: `mfem::Mesh` reports NV = 6160 after merging (1122 slaves), the sum of
   the element volumes equals the box volume (7.98129e6 nm3) and each grain has exactly the
   hexagonal-prism volume (1.99532e6 nm3). The box size for the periodic images of the bubbles
   is passed with `-lx/-ly/-lz` (defaults match the committed mesh).

## Bubbles: grain faces and grain edges

Like `test2`, the initial bubbles are analytic tanh spheres, but in 3D they sit either on the
grain faces or on the grain edges:

The initial bubbles follow Section 3.1 of the paper exactly (see above): 23 random grain-face
bubbles and 2 bubbles on each of the 8 triple lines, all spheres of radius 44 nm. The grain-face
triangles and triple lines are computed from a serial copy of the mesh (identical on every MPI
rank). The bubble field takes the 27 periodic images of each center (box size passed with
`-lx/-ly/-lz`, defaults matching the committed mesh).

## Run

```bash
cd build && make FissionGas3Dtest4 -j4
ctest -R "FissionGas3Dtest4"        # smoke test: -noamr -t 50 -dtmin 5 -dtmax 5, np 2
# production-style short run with AMR:
mpirun -np 4 tests/Studies/fissiongas/test4/FissionGas3Dtest4 -amr -elm_amr 2 -t 1e4
```

Outputs land in `Saves/Hex3D/` (VTU series with `eta_g1..eta_g4`, `eta_b`, `h_m`, `h_b`, `mu_v`,
`mu_g`, plus the `*_time_specialized.csv` tables compared by ctest). The smoke-test references in
`ref/` were generated from the committed `hex.msh` with exactly the ctest command.
